## What is Consent Form Builder?

Consent Form Builder is a free, browser-based tool from Poli International for creating custom consent and intake forms for tattoo and piercing studios. It runs entirely client-side: the builder makes no network requests other than loading its own translation file, so no client health data ever leaves the device.

The tool is a drag-and-drop form builder with three panels. On the left is a field palette with 17 field types grouped into categories (basic input, choice and options, layout and structure, notices and legal, clinical and media). In the centre is the canvas where you arrange fields, reorder them by dragging, and see a live count of fields placed. On the right is the properties editor, which opens when you click a field and lets you change its type, label, internal CRM alias, required status, and validation rules.

Beyond basic form building, the tool includes:

- **Eight complete templates**, each a full sectioned form rather than a skeleton, covering tattoo consent, piercing consent, medical history intake, multi-session projects, minor consent, cover-up assessment, PMU consultation, and a blank custom form.
- **Conditional logic** so a field only appears when a parent question has a particular answer, using operators such as equals, not equals, contains, is checked, greater than, and is empty.
- **A data integrity checker** that scans the form for circular logic dependencies, dropdowns with no options, duplicate option labels, inverted validation bounds, invalid regular expressions, duplicate CRM aliases, and missing labels.
- **A medical history system** with pre-built questions covering allergies, medical conditions, current medications, blood thinners, pregnancy, alcohol consumption, and eating within the last four hours.
- **An anatomical body map annotator** with four views (full body front and back, ear anatomy, facial anatomy, arms and hands) where you can drop numbered pins, draw freehand markings in five colours, and record placement specs such as gauge and jewellery material.
- **Digital signature capture** with a signing date stamp, plus PDF export with configurable paper size, margins, and a studio watermark or logo.
- **Tablet Kiosk Mode** that presents the form one step at a time with large touch targets.
- **CSV bulk import** to build fields from a spreadsheet, and **field ID mapping** exports for CRM and webhook integration.
- **Seven interface languages**: English, French, Italian, German, Spanish, Dutch, and Portuguese.

Everything you build is saved as a draft in the browser's local storage after every change, with an offer to restore it when you return.

## How to Use the Consent Form Builder

### Step 1: Pick a Template

Use the **Template** dropdown in the header. Selecting a template replaces the canvas with a complete, sectioned form. The eight options are:

| Template | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Tattoo Consent & Release Agreement | Standard tattoo appointments: studio and artist details, medical disclosure, release waiver. |
| Piercing Consent & Release | Piercing appointments: anatomy and placement, jewellery material and gauge, sterilisation lot. |
| Medical History & Health Screening Intake | A standalone health questionnaire when screening happens ahead of the appointment. |
| Multi-Session Tattoo Project Master Agreement | Large pieces booked across several sittings, with per-session tracking. |
| Minor Consent & Legal Guardian Authorization | Where local law permits work on a minor with a guardian present and co-signing. |
| Cover-Up & Rework Assessment | Assessing existing work before covering or reworking it. |
| PMU & Cosmetic Tattoo Consultation | Permanent make-up and cosmetic tattoo consultations. |
| Blank Custom Form | Starting from nothing. |

Templates are a drafting aid, not legal advice. Consent requirements differ by country, state, and municipality, so have your final wording reviewed by someone qualified in your jurisdiction.

### Step 2: Add and Arrange Fields on the Canvas

Drag field types from the left palette onto the canvas. Reorder fields by dragging them. The canvas toolbar gives you:

- **Grid toggle** to show a background alignment grid for precision drag-and-drop.
- **Smart Snap toggle** for magnetic alignment guidelines when dragging fields. It is on by default.
- **Auto-Sort** to sort fields alphabetically by label within all sections.
- **Integrity Checker** to open the diagnostic scan.
- A **field count badge** showing how many fields are currently placed.
- A **validation status pill** showing live field configuration status.

The palette has a search box (Ctrl+F) to filter field types, a **Smart Suggest** toggle that recommends fields your form is missing for the kind of work it describes, and a count badge showing the total available field types.

### Step 3: Configure Field Properties

Click a field on the canvas and the right panel switches from Form Overview to Field Properties. You can set:

- **Field type**, changeable in place without losing the label.
- **Field label**, the question as the client reads it.
- **Internal field alias (CRM / API key)**, a stable machine name such as `client_allergies_list`. Exports use the alias, so you can rename a question without breaking a spreadsheet or an integration.
- **Required**, which blocks submission and PDF generation until answered.
- **Validation**, including character limits, numeric bounds, and regular-expression patterns.

Select several fields with Shift + click to set required or hidden across all of them at once.

### Step 4: Set Up Conditional Logic

Any field can be shown only when another field has a particular answer, so a client answering "no" to a medical question never sees the six follow-ups underneath it. Set the parent question and the triggering value in the **Logic** section of the field's properties.

The logic engine supports these operators: equals, not equals, contains, not contains, is checked, is not checked, greater than, greater or equal, less than, less or equal, is empty, and is not empty. You can combine conditions with **show if all** or **show if any**.

Deeply chained rules can accidentally form a loop, where A depends on B and B depends back on A, leaving both permanently hidden. The integrity checker detects exactly that.

### Step 5: Run the Data Integrity Checker

Click **Integrity Checker** on the canvas toolbar to run a diagnostic scan and score the form. It catches the mistakes that only show up once a client is standing in front of you:

- Circular logic dependencies that make fields unreachable.
- Dropdowns, radios, and checkbox groups with no options configured.
- Duplicate option labels that make an answer ambiguous.
- Inverted validation bounds, such as a minimum above the maximum.
- Invalid regular-expression patterns.
- Duplicate CRM aliases, which collide on export.
- Missing labels and empty sections.

Each finding explains the consequence and offers **Jump to Field & Fix**, or **Quick-Fix** where the repair is unambiguous. **Auto-Fix Safe Issues** applies every unambiguous fix at once.

### Step 6: Preview the Client View and Safety Callouts

Click **Preview Form** to render the form exactly as a client sees it, with a completion meter showing how many mandatory fields remain.

As medical answers come in, the preview raises **safety callouts** above the form: disclosures that need artist awareness, a procedural precaution, or formal physician clearance. A disclosed latex allergy, for instance, produces a barrier alert naming the substitution required at that workstation.

**Fill Sample Answers** populates the form with test data so you can see the callouts and the PDF output before going live.

### Step 7: Capture Signatures and Export the PDF

The **Digital Signature** field accepts a mouse, stylus, or fingertip and stamps the signing date. **PDF & Print Settings** (under Options) controls the export: paper size, margins, and a studio watermark or logo.

**Print Form** opens the browser print dialog against a print stylesheet. Turn on **Print after Signature** if you want that to happen automatically the moment a client finishes signing.

**Export CSV** writes the responses out with the CRM aliases as column headers, ready for a spreadsheet or an import into other software.

### Step 8: Export, Import, and Map to Your CRM

The **Export** dropdown offers four options:

- **Export JSON Template** to save the full form schema file (Ctrl+S).
- **Download CSV Template** with form field labels as column headers for CRM migration.
- **Schema PDF Guide** explaining form structure and validation rules for clients.
- **Export Field IDs & CRM Map**, a visual variable map for HubSpot, Salesforce, and webhooks.

The **Import** dropdown offers two options:

- **Import JSON Template** to load a complete form schema JSON.
- **Bulk Import Fields (CSV)** to generate fields from a spreadsheet.

For bulk CSV import, upload a file or paste rows directly. Expected columns are `Label,Type,Required,HelperText,Options`:

```
Label,Type,Required,HelperText,Options
Emergency contact,text,true,Full legal name,
Preferred language,select,false,Pick your main language,English;French;Thai
Are you pregnant or breastfeeding?,radio,true,Required for safety,Yes;No
```

## Anatomical Body Map Annotator

The body map field provides interactive anatomical mapping for tattoo stencil placement, ear curation, facial piercing placement, gauge specifications, and skin condition notes. It is completely client-side with SVG vector diagrams, pin-drop coordinates, freehand sketching, and high-resolution PNG export for consultation records and PDF embedding.

**Four views:**

- Full Body (Front & Back)
- Ear Anatomy (Left & Right)
- Facial Anatomy & Oral
- Arms & Hands

**Tools:**

- **Pin** to drop a numbered placement pin on the diagram. When you drop a pin, the tool prompts for a placement specification and optional specs such as `16G ASTM F-136 Titanium 5/16" Labret` or `4x6 Stencil`.
- **Draw** for freehand marking or circling placement areas.
- **Five marker colours:** red marker, blue marker, black ink, green marker, purple stencil.
- **Undo** to remove the last mark or pin.
- **Clear** to remove all pins and markings from the diagram.

The tool suggests a label based on where you click. On the ear view, for example, clicking low on the diagram suggests "Lobe (Lower / Upper)", while clicking upper-left suggests "Helix / Industrial". On the face view, clicking the upper area suggests "Eyebrow / Forehead Placement", the middle suggests "Nostril / Septum / Bridge", and lower suggests "Philtrum / Medusa / Labret / Lip".

Each pin is listed in a markers panel with its number, label, notes, view, and coordinates, and can be removed individually.

## Conditional Logic and Dependency Rules

The logic engine evaluates field visibility and dependencies based on client responses and preceding selections. It runs across radio, select, checkbox, and text fields, and handles nested fields inside containers.

**Supported operators:**

| Operator | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| equals / eq | Matches the target value, including within arrays |
| not_equals / neq | Does not match the target value |
| contains | Value includes the target string |
| not_contains | Value does not include the target string |
| is_checked | True, "true", "yes", or a non-empty array |
| is_not_checked | Falsy, "false", "no", or an empty array |
| greater_than / gt | Numeric comparison |
| greater_equal / gte | Numeric comparison |
| less_than / lt | Numeric comparison |
| less_equal / lte | Numeric comparison |
| is_empty | Undefined, null, empty string, or empty array |
| is_not_empty | Has a value |

Conditions can be combined with **show if all** (every condition must pass) or **show if any** (at least one must pass).

## Data and Privacy

Nothing you type reaches Poli International's servers. The builder makes no network requests other than loading its own translation file. Forms, client answers, signatures, and generated PDFs are created and stored in your browser only.

Practical consequences worth understanding:

- Drafts and submissions live in this browser's local storage. They are not synced between machines, and a different browser or a private window will not see them.
- Clearing site data deletes them permanently. There is no server-side copy to restore.
- Because records stay on your device, you remain the data controller for them. Your obligations under GDPR, PDPA, or local health-records law are unchanged, and how you store the exported PDFs is your responsibility.

Export a JSON copy of any form template you rely on, and archive completed consent PDFs wherever you already keep client records.

## Use Case Examples

### Example 1: Standard Tattoo Appointment with Latex Allergy

A studio loads the **Tattoo Consent & Release Agreement** template. The client fills in studio and artist details, then reaches the medical disclosure section. Under **Allergies**, they check **Latex**. Under **Medical Conditions**, they leave everything unchecked.

Because the client answered the allergy question, the preview raises a safety callout above the form: a barrier alert naming the latex-free substitution required at that workstation. The artist sees this before the appointment starts.

The client signs with a fingertip on the studio tablet. The signing date is stamped automatically. The studio exports the PDF with the studio logo as a watermark and archives it alongside the client's other records.

### Example 2: Ear Piercing with Placement and Jewellery Specs

A piercer loads the **Piercing Consent & Release** template, which ships with 7 sections and 28 fields, 18 mandatory. The client completes the health screening, then the piercer opens the **Anatomical Body Map** field and switches to the **Ear Anatomy (Left & Right)** view.

The piercer drops a pin on the upper-left of the diagram. The tool suggests "Helix / Industrial" as the label. The piercer confirms it and adds specs: `16G ASTM F-136 Titanium 5/16" Labret`. A second pin goes on the lobe, labelled "Lobe (Lower / Upper)" with `14G Titanium 8mm Ball`.

The markers panel lists both pins with their numbers, labels, notes, view, and coordinates. The piercer exports the annotated diagram as a PNG and attaches it to the consultation record.

### Example 3: Multi-Session Tattoo Project

A client books a large back piece across four sittings. The studio loads the **Multi-Session Tattoo Project Master Agreement** template, which includes per-session tracking.

The studio sets up conditional logic so that a question about the previous session's healing only appears when the client is booking session two or later. A client booking their first session never sees it.

Before going live, the studio runs the **Integrity Checker**. It flags a duplicate CRM alias on two fields both named `session_notes`. The studio renames one to `session_notes_previous` and re-runs the check. The form scores clean.

The studio exports the **Field ID Map** as a sample webhook payload and hands it to their developer, who wires the form into the studio's booking system.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Is the Consent Form Builder free?**
Yes, with no account and no usage limit. You can also embed it on your own site at no cost.

**Does a digitally signed form hold up legally?**
Electronic signatures are widely recognised, but the standard of proof and the record-keeping requirements vary by jurisdiction. Treat the exported PDF as you would a signed paper form, and confirm the requirements that apply where you practise.

**Can I get my forms back on another computer?**
Only by moving them yourself. Use **Export → Export JSON** on the first machine and **Import → Import JSON** on the second.

**Why is a field greyed out or missing in the client view?**
Either it is hidden, or a conditional rule is keeping it hidden because its parent question has not been answered with the triggering value. Run **Validate Form**; if the parent no longer exists, the checker reports it as orphaned logic.

**The PDF is missing my logo.**
Upload it under **Options → PDF & Print Settings**. Like everything else, it is stored in this browser, so it needs uploading again on another machine.

**Can I collect submissions online?**
Not through Poli International, by design. The tool has no backend. If you need submissions delivered somewhere, use **Export → Field ID Map** to get the schema and sample webhook payload, and wire the form into your own system.

**How many field types are there?**
Seventeen, grouped into basic input, choice and options, layout and structure, notices and legal, and clinical and media.

**Can I build a form for a minor?**
Yes. The **Minor Consent & Legal Guardian Authorization** template is designed for cases where local law permits work on a minor with a guardian present and co-signing. Confirm the requirements that apply in your jurisdiction before using it.

**What happens if I accidentally delete a field?**
Deleted fields go to a **Recently Deleted** bin at the bottom of the canvas, so an accidental deletion is recoverable even after several more edits.

**Does the tool work offline?**
The builder runs entirely in your browser and makes no network requests other than loading its own translation file. Once that file is loaded, the tool works without a connection.

**Can I translate a form for a client who speaks another language?**
Yes. The interface and templates are available in English, French, Italian, German, Spanish, Dutch, and Portuguese. Switching the language translates both the builder's controls and the field labels of the form you are building. Fields you have written yourself stay exactly as you typed them.

**How do I move an existing paper form into the builder?**
Use **Import → Bulk Import CSV** and upload a spreadsheet with columns `Label,Type,Required,HelperText,Options`. You can also paste rows directly.
